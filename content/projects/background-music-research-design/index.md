## Overview

This project was developed as part of a Research Methods course in my Bachelor's degree in Psychology. It involved developing a research proposal to investigate the effects of background music on cognitive performance and concentration during brain-game tasks.

The proposal explores whether background music can influence users' performance and ability to maintain attention while completing cognitive tasks in brain-game applications. It draws on theories of attention, cognitive capacity, and arousal, as well as previous research on music and cognitive performance.

## Research Objectives

The primary objective is to investigate the effect of background music on performance and concentration in brain-game applications.

The specific objectives are to:

- Examine the effect of background music on task performance.
- Investigate the effect of background music on concentration during task execution.

## Research Questions and Hypotheses

The proposal examines whether background music has a significant effect on performance and concentration during brain-game tasks.

The main research hypothesis is that background music affects both outcomes. The secondary hypotheses address its effects on concentration and task performance separately, including the possibility of improved outcomes.

## Theoretical Background

The proposal draws on cognitive psychology frameworks, particularly:

- **Kahneman's Capacity Theory of Attention:** to consider how background music may compete with a cognitive task for limited processing resources.
- **The Yerkes–Dodson Law:** to consider how music-induced arousal may influence performance.
- **Research on auditory distraction and cognitive performance:** to examine how music may affect attention, working memory, and task execution.

The literature review incorporates studies on musical tempo during gameplay, preferred music during puzzle-solving, music during stressful decision-making tasks, and the effects of background music on attention and memory.

## Research Methodology

The proposed study uses a controlled, two-group design to compare participants completing a brain-game task under experimental and control conditions.

- **Study design:** A quasi-experimental design with an experimental group and a control group, as specified in the original proposal.
- **Target population:** Adults aged 18 and above.
- **Sampling method:** Voluntary and purposive recruitment, followed by random assignment to the experimental and control groups.
- **Proposed sample size:** 100 participants, with 50 participants per group, accounting for potential attrition.
- **Experimental condition:** Participants perform the task with background music.
- **Control condition:** Participants perform the task without background music.

The proposal also identifies potential confounding, moderating, and control variables, including prior gaming experience, sleep, environmental noise, working memory, attentional ability, music preference, music type, game difficulty, and music volume.

## Variables and Operationalization

**Independent variable**
- Background music condition, operationalized through the type of music presented during task performance.

**Dependent variables**
- **Task performance:** The score earned according to the rules of the brain game.
- **Concentration:** An operational measure based on participants' reported instances of selecting an incorrect option despite knowing the correct answer. The proportion of such reports relative to total selections is subtracted from one to obtain the proposed concentration score.

The proposal distinguishes concentration from overall task performance by attempting to measure errors attributed to lapses in attention separately from the score earned in the game.

## Data Collection and Analysis Considerations

The proposed data collection relies on behavioral measures recorded during task execution rather than questionnaires administered after the task. The proposal also identifies statistical control of working memory and attention using analysis of covariance (ANCOVA), alongside standardization of music volume.

## Significance

The study has potential theoretical and practical relevance.

- **Theoretical significance:** It may contribute to understanding the relationship between auditory stimulation, attentional resources, emotional arousal, and cognitive performance.
- **Practical significance:** Its findings could inform the design of brain-training applications, educational tools, and digital environments intended to support concentration and task performance.
- **Organizational significance:** The findings may also be relevant to educational settings, workplaces, and cognitive rehabilitation contexts where background music is used.

These are proposed applications; the project does not report experimental findings.

This project provided an opportunity to apply core research-methods concepts in psychology, including:

- Developing a research question and formulating hypotheses.
- Identifying independent, dependent, confounding, moderating, and control variables.
- Defining constructs theoretically and operationally.
- Reviewing and synthesizing relevant literature.
- Selecting a research design and sampling strategy.
